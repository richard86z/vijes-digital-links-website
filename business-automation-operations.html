AUTOMATION IN PRACTICE

Improve the process before automating it.

The best starting point is a real operational task that people repeat and can describe. Document the trigger, the information required, the steps, the decisions, the person responsible and the expected result before selecting a tool.

01 · MAP

Understand the current workflow

Identify hand-offs, approvals, duplicate data entry, common delays and exceptions. Ask the people who perform the work to confirm that the process map reflects what actually happens.

02 · ASSESS

Choose a suitable task

Look for frequent, rule-based work with clear inputs and outcomes. Compare expected time savings with setup, subscription, maintenance and exception-handling costs.

03 · CONTROL

Keep people responsible

Define permissions, approval points, failure alerts and a manual fallback. Keep human review for decisions involving money, legal duties, sensitive information or customer commitments.

Examples of practical workflow improvements

Depending on the tools already in use, a project might assess enquiry routing, task creation from a submitted form, routine acknowledgements, deadline reminders, approved data transfer or recurring report preparation. These are examples for evaluation, not a promise that every system can be connected or automated.

Before a workflow goes live, test normal and unusual cases: missing fields, repeated submissions, invalid data, delayed services, failed notifications and permission errors. Compare the result with the expected outcome, document how to pause the automation and identify who monitors it.

Deliverables may include a process map, improvement recommendations, automation requirements, a tested workflow, user instructions and a maintenance checklist. Client approval, data protection, account ownership and access controls should be agreed in advance. A simpler checklist or clearer responsibility may sometimes be better than adding another software tool.
