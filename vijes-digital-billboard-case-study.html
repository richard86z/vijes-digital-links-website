Building a digital advertising billboard: the Vijes project

A behind-the-scenes account of how Vijes Digital Links approached a practical advertising problem: giving businesses a public place to present a promotion while creating a review and campaign-management process behind the scenes.

Published 20 November 2026 · Project owner: Vijes Digital Links · Project type: internally developed platform

Scope and transparency: This is a case study of a Vijes-owned project, not a commissioned client campaign. It documents the platform's design and intended workflow. It does not claim independently verified advertiser sales, reach or return on investment.

1. The problem the project set out to address

Small businesses, service providers and organisations often need a simple way to put a promotion in front of an audience. At the same time, an advertising platform needs more than a page that displays images. It needs a way to collect campaign information, review submissions, clarify requirements, confirm payment where applicable, schedule publication and understand whether visitors interact with an advert.

Vijes Digital Links developed its Digital Billboard as an internal platform project to explore that end-to-end workflow. The public-facing page is intended to present approved promotional placements. A separate advertiser journey collects campaign requests, while an administration workflow supports review and publication decisions.

2. Designing the advertiser journey

The advertiser portal is designed to collect the information needed to assess a campaign request, including the advertiser's details, promotion, category, target market, preferred placement and destination. An advertiser can provide an image for review, subject to the portal's file requirements.

This intake step matters because a campaign cannot be assessed from a banner image alone. The platform needs to understand what is being promoted, who it is for, where the advert should lead and when the campaign is intended to run. Clear information reduces avoidable back-and-forth and helps the publisher identify missing details before publication.

3. Why review and approval are part of the design

The public billboard feed is designed to show campaigns that have passed the required review and publication checks. The system distinguishes a request from an approved advert: submitting a request does not itself guarantee acceptance, payment confirmation or publication.

The administration workflow is intended to support review of campaign details, quotations, payment verification, scheduling and publication status. This is an important operational control. A public advertising page should not automatically treat every uploaded image or submitted request as an approved campaign.

4. Campaign measurement: useful signals, not promises

The platform includes a mechanism for recording billboard interactions such as impressions and clicks. These measures can help an advertiser understand whether a placement was displayed and whether visitors followed its destination link. They are not, by themselves, proof of a sale, qualified lead or positive return on investment.

For meaningful evaluation, the advertiser should compare platform activity with the destination experience: website visits, enquiry submissions, calls, purchases or other agreed actions. Campaign dates, audience, creative, destination and the measurement method should be recorded so the results can be interpreted fairly.

Measurement distinction: An impression is a recorded display; a click is an interaction with the advert. Neither should be described as a customer, conversion or sale unless a separate, reliable measurement confirms that outcome.

5. Decisions made during development

  • Separate public display from administration: visitors see the public billboard, while campaign review and management take place through a separate workflow.
  • Collect structured campaign information: category, audience, dates and destination help make requests easier to assess.
  • Keep approval explicit: a submitted request is not automatically a published advert.
  • Use clear publication states: review, payment verification and scheduling should be distinguishable from public availability.
  • Measure activity carefully: impressions and clicks are useful indicators, but commercial outcomes require additional tracking.

6. What the project does not claim

The billboard project should not be presented as proof that every advertiser will receive a particular number of views, leads or sales. Audience size, campaign placement, creative quality, market demand, timing and the destination website can all affect results. A campaign quotation or request is not a guarantee of performance.

The platform is an evolving Vijes-owned project. Advertisers should confirm the available package, placement, dates, price, payment status, publication conditions and reporting arrangements with Vijes before relying on a campaign booking.

7. What comes next

Further development and testing should focus on making the advertiser instructions clearer, confirming that every public campaign has the correct approval and payment status, checking that campaign dates are respected, and reconciling recorded impressions and clicks with the campaign report. A complete operational test should also verify the journey from request through review, publication, campaign expiry and reporting.

These checks are more useful than simply adding features. They help make the platform understandable to advertisers and manageable for the team operating it.

8. What another organisation can learn from this project

Any organisation building an advertising or listing platform can use a similar sequence: define what a submission must contain, separate intake from approval, state what payment does and does not confirm, make publication rules visible, and measure the activity that can genuinely be observed. The process should be understandable to both the customer and the people administering it.

For a small business planning its own campaign, the same principles apply in reverse: prepare a clear offer, use an appropriate image, choose a relevant audience, provide a working destination and decide in advance what result would make the campaign worthwhile.
