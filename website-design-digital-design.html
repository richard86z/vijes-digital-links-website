WEBSITE DESIGN IN PRACTICE

Design around the visitor's journey.

A website is more than a visual layout. Its navigation, page content, forms, images and technical behaviour must work together. The starting point is to decide what the site needs to help visitors understand or do.

01 · STRUCTURE

Organise information logically

Group related information, use descriptive page titles and headings, and make important pages reachable without confusing menus. Each page should answer a clear visitor question.

02 · EXPERIENCE

Make pages usable on mobile

Check readable text, adequate contrast, keyboard-friendly controls, meaningful image descriptions, clear buttons and layouts that adapt to smaller screens.

03 · DELIVERY

Test the complete journey

Review links, enquiry forms, confirmation messages, images, page loading and contact routes. Test on more than one screen size before the site is treated as ready.

What to settle before design begins

Agree the primary audience, the site's purpose, required pages, content owner, brand assets, forms, contact channels, language needs and any integrations. Decide who will supply accurate text and images, who approves changes and who will maintain the website after handover.

Depending on the brief, deliverables can include a site map, page wireframe or content outline, responsive page design, copy support, basic on-page search metadata, enquiry forms and a handover note. Search optimisation is not a guarantee of rankings; it begins with accessible, relevant pages and technically sound basics.

Access credentials and API keys should be handled securely and never placed in public website code. A project should also identify responsibility for hosting, domain renewal, backups, software updates, privacy notices and future content changes. Third-party platforms and paid services may have separate charges that are confirmed before the work starts.
