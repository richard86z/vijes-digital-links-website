CONTENT & EMAIL IN PRACTICE

Make every message useful and clear.

Strong communication starts with the reader's question. Before drafting, clarify what the audience already knows, what information is missing, what action is appropriate and which details must be accurate.

01 · WEBSITE CONTENT

Explain the offer clearly

Organise page copy around the audience's needs: the problem addressed, the service or product offered, how the process works, important limitations and the best next step.

02 · EMAIL CAMPAIGNS

Respect permission and relevance

Plan messages for people who have agreed to receive them. Set expectations about topics and frequency, use an identifiable sender and provide a working way to unsubscribe.

03 · EDITORIAL QUALITY

Check facts and consistency

Review names, dates, prices, links, claims, tone and calls to action. Use approved brand information and distinguish confirmed results from estimates or illustrative examples.

From brief to campaign

A typical content process begins with a short brief covering the audience, purpose, offer, source material, tone, format, deadline and approval contact. A draft is prepared for review, corrections are agreed and the final version is checked in the format where it will appear. For email, test links, mobile readability, sender identity and the unsubscribe process before sending.

Useful deliverables may include website page copy, landing-page messaging, editorial calendars, newsletters, automated email sequences, promotional copy and an editing checklist. The scope should identify who provides product facts, legal notices, images, audience lists and platform access.

Do not buy or scrape email lists or assume that an existing contact has agreed to marketing. List permission, privacy notices, sender authentication and the rules applicable to recipients' locations should be addressed before campaigns are sent. Sending platform configuration and campaign delivery are included only when agreed; no message can guarantee inbox placement or conversions.
