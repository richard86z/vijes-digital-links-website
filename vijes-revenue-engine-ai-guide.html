Vijes Revenue Engine AI: from business research to opportunities

Finding potential clients can involve scattered searches, disconnected notes and uncertain assumptions about what a business may need. Vijes Revenue Engine AI is being developed around a more structured workflow: research organisations, inspect evidence, assess service fit and organise follow-through.

Published 20 November 2026 · Vijes Digital Links

Vijes Revenue Engine AI is an innovation project from Vijes Digital Links. Its intended audience includes digital marketing agencies, web and technology studios, consultants and independent service providers that need a more organised way to research potential clients and manage business-development activity.

The central idea is to connect steps that are often handled separately. A team may search for companies, collect information from different sources, try to understand a possible business need, decide whether its services are relevant and then manage outreach in another tool. The Revenue Engine concept aims to bring these activities into a connected workflow so that users can make more informed decisions and keep track of what happens next.

Development status: Vijes Revenue Engine AI is an ongoing product-development project. It is not yet complete or generally available. This article describes the product direction and intended capabilities, not a guarantee that every feature is finished or independently verified.

1. Start with a defined service and target market

Useful prospect research begins with a clear question. A service provider should define what it can deliver, which organisations it wants to reach, what geography or region is relevant and what type of business need may fit its offer.

For example, a web studio may be interested in organisations that need a new website or improvements to an existing digital experience. A marketing agency may focus on businesses that could benefit from clearer positioning, content, advertising or customer engagement. The service provider should be able to explain its own capability before deciding whether a prospect is a fit.

Vijes Revenue Engine AI is designed to support this initial definition through research inputs such as business category, industry, region and service focus. A focused search is generally more useful than trying to identify every possible company in every market.

2. Discover candidate organisations

The research experience is intended to find candidate organisations from selected markets and categories and organise the information for review. The purpose is to make discovery more structured than keeping disconnected search results in separate browser tabs or notes.

A candidate organisation is only a starting point. Its appearance in a research result does not automatically mean it is seeking a supplier, has a confirmed budget, is ready to buy or is an appropriate lead. Users need to review the available information and decide whether further research is justified.

3. Inspect evidence before accepting a business signal

One important design principle is that a possible opportunity should be supported by evidence that a user can inspect. Business signals may include a public announcement about expansion, a new product launch, procurement activity, a partnership or signs of customer growth. Such events can suggest a possible need, but they do not prove that an organisation intends to purchase a particular service.

Before treating a signal as useful, a researcher should ask:

  • Is there a source link that opens and supports the statement?
  • Is the source the organisation itself, a recognised publication, a public procurement source or another identifiable publisher?
  • Is the information recent enough for the decision being made?
  • Does the source actually say what the summary claims?
  • Does the signal relate to the service being offered, or is the connection only an assumption?
  • Is there conflicting or more recent information that changes the interpretation?

A source link is not proof by itself. Users should open the source, check its date and context, and record uncertainty. If a link cannot be verified or does not support the claim, the item should not be presented as a confirmed business opportunity.

4. Assess service fit rather than simply scoring company size

Opportunity scoring and provider matching are intended to compare a possible business need with the services a provider can actually deliver. A large or well-known organisation is not automatically a good prospect. A smaller company with a clear, relevant need may be a better fit.

A practical fit review considers the match between the organisation's apparent situation and the provider's capabilities, the strength of supporting evidence, the likely decision process, geographic and delivery constraints, and whether the provider can offer a credible next step.

Any numerical score should be treated as a prioritisation aid, not a prediction that a sale will occur. Users should be able to understand the reasons behind a score and challenge assumptions that are incomplete or incorrect.

5. Organise the journey from research to outreach

Once a candidate has been reviewed, a business-development team may decide to save a research note, request more information, prepare a proposal or begin an appropriate conversation. The planned pipeline connects prospects and opportunities with contacts, proposals and outreach activity.

Good outreach should be relevant, honest and respectful. It should explain why the sender is contacting the organisation, avoid pretending that a relationship already exists and make it easy for the recipient to decline further communication. Teams should follow applicable privacy, direct-marketing and anti-spam rules in the recipient's location. A public business email address should not automatically be treated as consent for every type of marketing.

Before sending a proposal, confirm the organisation's need through a genuine conversation or reliable request. Do not state that a company has a problem, budget or buying intention unless there is evidence for that statement.

6. Record outcomes and learn from them

Research is more useful when teams record what happened afterwards. A prospect may reply, decline, request more information, book a meeting, receive a proposal, become a customer or go quiet. Recording outcomes can help a team understand which types of research and outreach are producing relevant conversations.

The product direction includes sales-outcome and revenue tracking as a feedback loop. This is intended to help users review wins, losses and engagement patterns and improve future targeting. It should support human judgement rather than encourage automated assumptions about an organisation or an individual.

7. The planned platform areas

The current product plan describes connected areas that support different stages of the workflow. Their scope and availability may change during development and testing.

  • AI Research Engine: Supports structured business and market research and exploration of companies, industries and potential demand.
  • AI Sales Engine: Designed to help move from researched prospects towards relevant business conversations.
  • Advanced Intelligence Signals: Explores events such as launches, expansion, procurement, partnerships and customer growth that may indicate a possible need.
  • Research Studio and Live Prospect Finder: Guided discovery across business categories and regions.
  • Business Graph and Provider Matching: Connects companies, providers, needs, opportunities, proposals and match history.
  • AI Opportunity Agent: Intended to identify and organise opportunities from selected target markets and service areas, with review before prospects are treated as qualified pipeline records.
  • CRM, Outreach and Proposals: Helps organise contact records, pipeline stages, proposals and follow-up activity.
  • Sales Outcome and Revenue Learning: Provides a way to record results and review patterns over time.
  • Service Provider Marketplace: Being developed around service categories including digital marketing, websites, automation, outsourcing, content, research and language services.

8. What the product should not replace

An AI-assisted research tool cannot replace direct confirmation from a prospective customer, professional judgement, due diligence or knowledge of the service being sold. It should not be treated as an authority on a company's confidential plans, financial condition or purchasing intentions unless those facts are supported by reliable evidence.

Users remain responsible for checking source material, evaluating whether a proposed contact is appropriate, following applicable laws and ensuring that their own service claims are accurate. Automated summaries and scores can be incomplete or wrong and should be reviewed before being used in business decisions.

9. Development priorities and quality checks

The development work described by Vijes Digital Links includes refining research and opportunity workflows, improving evidence and source verification, connecting matching and pipeline records, strengthening owner-access and subscription behaviour, and testing the complete journey from discovery to outcome tracking.

For a product of this kind, quality checks should include whether source links open and support the claim, whether duplicate organisations are handled sensibly, whether users can correct inaccurate records, whether access permissions protect business data, and whether pipeline stages reflect real user actions. Testing should also examine failure cases, not only successful demonstrations.

Any public description of the product should distinguish planned functions from features that have been implemented and tested. Release timing, pricing, supported countries and feature availability should be confirmed through official product information when the platform is ready.

10. Who may find the concept useful?

The project is being designed for service providers whose work depends on finding and understanding potential clients. This includes digital marketing agencies, web and technology studios, consultants, specialists and independent providers. Its usefulness will depend on the quality of the available information, the accuracy of its matching and the way each team uses the workflow.

For a small provider, a structured research process may help reduce scattered notes and make follow-up more consistent. For a larger team, connected records may help people share context and review pipeline activity. These are intended benefits, not measured performance claims or guarantees of increased revenue.

How Vijes Digital Links came to develop the project

Vijes Digital Links works across digital marketing, content and email marketing, social media management, website services, automation, market research, translation and interpretation, and business support. The Revenue Engine AI project reflects an effort to connect business research and service-provider development into a more organised digital workflow.

The project is being developed as part of the company's broader interest in helping service providers identify relevant opportunities and manage business growth more systematically. Its public release will depend on further implementation, verification and testing.

The design has grown from the needs of a service business that must decide where to look, which organisations deserve further research and how to connect an observed business issue to a service it can deliver. The Live Prospect Finder is being shaped for different organisation types and sectors—including B2B companies, SaaS, e-commerce, education, real estate, NGOs, healthcare, finance and professional services—and for research across global regions. These filters help define the research question; they do not prove that a selected organisation has a buying need.

A second design decision is to connect research records with service capabilities and follow-up. The Business Graph brings companies, providers, needs, opportunities, proposals and match history into a shared model. The intended benefit is traceability: a user should be able to understand which organisation is being considered, which need is relevant, what evidence supports that need, which service may fit and what action followed.

The product's most important quality test is not how many names an AI can generate. It is whether a user can verify the source, detect an irrelevant or stale signal, correct an organisation record, explain a match, and avoid saving unsupported claims as confirmed opportunities. Development work therefore includes source-link checks, record accuracy, access and subscription testing, and validation of the route from research to a managed pipeline. Those workflows are still being refined; the project does not claim measured sales growth or proven return on investment.

Opportunity evidence review: a practical checklist

The Revenue Engine AI concept is intended to organise research and opportunity workflows. An AI-generated match is a lead to investigate—not proof that an organisation has a current need or intends to buy.

  1. Identity: Confirm the organisation's official name, website and relevant operating region.
  2. Source: Open the original source page. Record its URL, publisher, publication date and the exact claim it supports.
  3. Recency: Check whether the signal is current enough for the proposed outreach.
  4. Need: Distinguish an explicit procurement notice or request from a general news item, hiring signal or inferred business challenge.
  5. Fit: Explain which Vijes service could address the verified need and why.
  6. Contact: Use an appropriate public business contact route and respect privacy and marketing rules.
  7. Decision: Mark verified, needs review or reject; record reviewer, date and reason.

Evidence record: organisation ___; opportunity claim ___; source URL ___; source date ___; evidence excerpt/summary ___; confidence and limitation ___; proposed service ___; next human check ___.

Do not save an opportunity as verified if the link is inaccessible, the source does not support the claim, or the organisation identity is uncertain. Keep human review before outreach and record outcomes so the process can learn from corrections.
