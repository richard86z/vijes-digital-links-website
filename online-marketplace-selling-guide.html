How to prepare your business to sell on an online marketplace

An online marketplace can help sellers reach customers, but a listing alone does not create a reliable selling operation. Sellers need accurate product information, realistic pricing, dependable fulfilment and a clear way to handle customer questions and problems.

Published 20 November 2026 · Editorial team: Vijes Digital Links

An online marketplace brings multiple sellers and buyers together through a shared platform. Depending on the marketplace, it may support physical products, digital products or services. The platform may provide product discovery, seller profiles, checkout tools, order records, customer communication or other services. Sellers should understand exactly what the platform does—and what remains their responsibility—before accepting orders.

This guide is a general preparation checklist. Marketplace rules, fees, payment timing, delivery arrangements and consumer obligations differ, so always read the current terms for the platform and the locations where you trade.

1. Check whether the marketplace fits your offer

Review the platform's product categories, customer audience, permitted items, seller eligibility and operating countries. Check whether your product or service is allowed and whether any licence, certification, safety document or professional qualification is required.

Understand the commercial terms before investing time in listings. Look for commissions, subscription fees, listing charges, payment processing fees, advertising costs, payout schedules, reserve policies, refund rules and dispute procedures. Ask how the platform handles orders when a payment is reversed or a customer reports a problem.

Do not assume that every marketplace supports the same payment methods, currencies or delivery locations. Confirm availability for the seller's country and the buyer's destination.

2. Prepare your seller information

Use accurate business and contact information. Depending on the platform and jurisdiction, verification may require identity, business registration, tax or bank-account details. Submit information only through the platform's official secure process and make sure the details are consistent.

Set up a monitored customer-service email address and a clear process for responding to marketplace messages. Decide who can access the seller account and use individual authorised logins where available. Protect the account with strong authentication and never share one-time passcodes or passwords with someone claiming to be platform support.

3. Create a useful product or service listing

A listing should help a buyer understand what is being offered and decide whether it meets their needs. Use a clear title that identifies the item or service rather than filling it with repeated search terms.

For physical products, provide accurate details such as:

  • Brand, model, size, material, colour and other relevant specifications.
  • Condition, quantity, included accessories and any known defects.
  • Dimensions, weight and compatibility where relevant.
  • Clear photographs that show the actual product from useful angles.
  • Care, safety, warranty or usage information where applicable.
  • Dispatch location, estimated handling time and delivery limitations.

For services, describe the scope, deliverables, process, expected timeframe, customer inputs, revision limits and exclusions. Explain whether the price is fixed or requires a quote. Do not promise outcomes that depend on factors outside the seller's control.

Use photographs and text that you own or are authorised to use. Do not use another seller's images, logos or product descriptions without permission. Make sure images represent the item being sold and do not hide material defects.

4. Set a price that accounts for the full cost

Calculate the costs involved in fulfilling an order before setting the displayed price. Depending on the business, these may include product or production cost, packaging, labour, platform commission, payment processing, shipping, taxes, returns, customer support and promotional discounts.

A simple pricing worksheet can include:

  • Expected selling price and currency.
  • Cost of the item or service delivery.
  • Marketplace and payment fees.
  • Packaging, fulfilment and shipping costs.
  • Expected taxes or duties, where applicable.
  • Expected return, replacement or support costs.
  • Estimated amount remaining after the listed costs.

Distinguish revenue from profit. A sale value is not the same as the amount a seller keeps after costs and fees. Check whether the marketplace deducts commission from the item price, shipping, tax or another amount, and whether fees are refundable when an order is cancelled.

5. Make inventory and fulfilment reliable

Keep stock quantities current and avoid accepting orders for items that are unavailable. If the same inventory is listed on multiple channels, use a process to synchronise stock or promptly update listings when a sale occurs elsewhere.

Before publishing a listing, decide how orders will be picked, checked, packed and dispatched. Use packaging suitable for the item and the expected journey. Provide realistic handling and delivery estimates rather than choosing the fastest option simply to attract a sale.

For services or digital products, define how work begins after purchase, what information the buyer must supply, how delivery is confirmed and how completion is recorded. Keep appropriate evidence of fulfilment without collecting more customer data than necessary.

6. Communicate clearly with buyers

Respond to questions using the marketplace's approved communication tools. Give accurate answers about availability, specifications, delivery, service scope and return conditions. If you do not know an answer, verify it before replying.

Send updates when an order is accepted, prepared, dispatched or ready for the next service milestone, where the platform supports those updates. If a delay occurs, tell the buyer promptly and explain the available options. Good communication cannot prevent every problem, but it can reduce confusion and help resolve issues earlier.

7. Understand delivery, returns and disputes

Read the marketplace's rules for cancellations, returns, refunds, damaged goods, non-delivery and chargebacks. These policies may interact with local consumer law, which can provide rights that platform terms cannot remove.

Keep order records, dispatch evidence, delivery confirmation and relevant customer communications in the approved system. If a buyer reports a problem, follow the platform's process and respond within the stated deadline. Do not ask a buyer to close a dispute or make an off-platform payment in exchange for assistance.

For cross-border sales, clarify who is responsible for shipping, customs documentation, duties, import restrictions and returns. Some products cannot legally be shipped to every destination. Obtain qualified advice where product safety, export controls, tax or consumer rules apply.

8. Protect the seller account and customers

Use unique passwords and multi-factor authentication where available. Restrict staff permissions to the tasks they need. Be cautious of messages asking you to click an unfamiliar link, pay an unexpected release fee or disclose account credentials.

Keep customer information within approved systems and use it only for legitimate order and service purposes. Do not export or reuse buyer contact details for unrelated marketing unless the buyer has given the required permission and the activity is permitted by law and platform rules.

Be transparent about product origin, condition, service capabilities, delivery estimates and limitations. Avoid counterfeit goods, misleading claims, fake reviews, artificial orders or attempts to move transactions outside the platform to evade fees or protections.

9. Monitor performance and improve the listing

Review more than the number of views. Useful seller measures may include:

  • Listing views: Whether buyers are discovering the offer.
  • Conversion rate: The proportion of relevant visits that result in an order, using the platform's stated definition.
  • Order completion: Whether accepted orders are fulfilled successfully.
  • Cancellation and return rate: How often orders fail or are returned, and why.
  • Response time: How promptly buyer questions are handled.
  • Customer feedback: Recurring themes in ratings, reviews and support requests.
  • Net contribution: What remains after fees, fulfilment costs, refunds and other relevant expenses.

Use feedback to improve descriptions, photographs, stock accuracy, packaging and customer communication. Do not manipulate reviews or pressure buyers to provide positive feedback. If a platform's metric definition is unclear, consult its current seller documentation before comparing results.

10. A seller launch checklist

  1. Platform fit: Confirm that the offer and seller location are eligible.
  2. Verification: Complete required seller and payment checks through the official platform.
  3. Commercial terms: Understand commissions, fees, payout timing, refunds and disputes.
  4. Listing quality: Prepare accurate descriptions, specifications and authorised images.
  5. Pricing: Calculate costs and check the amount expected after fees.
  6. Operations: Test inventory, packaging, delivery or service fulfilment processes.
  7. Customer support: Set response responsibilities and escalation steps.
  8. Security: Protect accounts and limit access to authorised people.
  9. Review: Check the listing and policies before accepting the first order.
Before accepting your first order: Make sure you can identify the buyer's order, confirm the agreed item or service, fulfil it within the stated timeframe, provide the required evidence, handle a complaint and understand when and how the marketplace releases your proceeds.
