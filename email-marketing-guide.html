EMAIL MARKETING · PRACTICAL GUIDE

Email marketing that earns attention and builds trust

Email can help an organisation stay useful and recognisable between customer interactions. Strong campaigns respect permission, deliver relevant information and make it easy for readers to choose what they receive.

Published 11 November 2026 · By Vijes Digital Links

Email marketing is not simply sending promotions to a large list. It is a communication channel built on permission and trust. When people understand why they are receiving messages and consistently find them useful, email can support education, customer relationships, event participation and sales conversations.

1. Build a list the right way

Collect addresses from people who have clearly agreed to receive your communications. Explain what they are signing up for, how often you expect to email and how they can unsubscribe. Keep a record of consent and its source. Do not buy, scrape or borrow mailing lists, and do not assume that having someone's business card or email address means they agreed to marketing messages.

2. Set expectations at sign-up

A good sign-up experience tells people what they will receive. Be specific: monthly business insights, product updates, event announcements or occasional service information. Ask only for information you genuinely need. If you offer different topics, consider allowing subscribers to choose their interests.

3. Give each campaign one main purpose

Before writing, decide what the email should help the reader understand or do. A newsletter may share a short collection of useful resources. A service update may explain a change. An event email may provide the details and registration step. A promotional message may introduce an offer and its terms.

One clear purpose helps keep the subject line, content and call to action aligned. Avoid adding several unrelated promotions to a single email simply because you have a list to contact.

4. Write for the reader's time

Use a recognisable sender name, a specific subject line and an opening that quickly explains why the message matters. Break longer messages into short sections with descriptive headings. Use plain language and explain specialist terms. Make links descriptive so readers know what they will open.

Keep the design readable on mobile devices. Use accessible contrast, legible text and meaningful alternative text for important images. Ensure the message still makes sense if images are blocked.

5. Protect deliverability and sender reputation

Use a reputable email service and configure the domain authentication it recommends, commonly SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Send to people who expect your messages. Remove invalid addresses and honour unsubscribes promptly. Avoid misleading subject lines, sudden unexplained volume spikes and attachments that recipients did not request.

Authentication helps receiving systems verify that messages are authorised by your domain; it does not guarantee inbox placement. Monitor bounces, spam complaints and engagement, and investigate problems rather than repeatedly mailing an unresponsive list.

6. Make the call to action clear

Tell the reader what the next step is. Use one primary action where possible, such as reading a guide, registering for an event, replying with a question or viewing a service page. Make sure the destination page matches the promise made in the email.

7. Measure more than opens

Useful measures include delivery and bounce rates, unsubscribes, spam complaints, clicks, replies, completed registrations, qualified enquiries and sales outcomes. Open-rate data can be affected by privacy features and automated image loading, so do not use it as the sole indicator of success.

Compare similar campaigns over time. A small, relevant list that generates meaningful conversations may be more valuable than a large list with little engagement.

8. A simple campaign checklist

  • Is the recipient list permission-based and appropriate for this message?
  • Does the sender name clearly identify the organisation?
  • Does the subject line accurately describe the content?
  • Is there one clear purpose and a useful call to action?
  • Do all links work and lead to the expected page?
  • Does the email display well on a phone?
  • Is the organisation's contact information visible?
  • Is there a working unsubscribe option, and will it be honoured?
  • Have you checked the message for accuracy, spelling and misleading claims?

Final thought

Trust is the foundation of sustainable email marketing. Send less noise and more relevant information. Make consent, clarity and reader choice part of the process, then use campaign results to improve the next message.
